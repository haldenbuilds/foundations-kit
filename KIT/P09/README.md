# P09 | Offer and best-customer sheet

**Row ID:** `P09`

**Built tier:** `fully kitted`

**Purpose:** Offer and best-customer sheet

**Use after:** P01,C07

## Populated first instance

**What it does:** Write down who buys, what they get, what it costs them, and why it beats doing nothing, on one page.

**Starter scenario:** Offer sheet for the neutral document-preparation service described in P01.

This first instance is populated with neutral, explicit starter content. Recipient-specific facts not supplied to the package remain `UNKNOWN` and must be confirmed before they are treated as authority.

| Surface | Populated content |
|---|---|
| Buyer, in one sentence | A small team that needs recurring project updates (from P01). |
| Problem they pay to remove | Current status is scattered across notes and decisions (from P01). |
| Result they want, in their words | UNKNOWN until a buyer says it. Write their words, not yours. |
| What they receive | A source-linked draft update for owner review, plus an evidence checklist (from P01). |
| Price and how they pay | UNKNOWN until the owner sets it. P10 works out what it earns. |
| What they compare you to | Writing the update themselves, asking a general assistant, or doing nothing and living with scattered status. |
| Friction check | Four questions, asked from the buyer's side. How big is the result? How sure are they that it will work for them? How long until they see it? How much effort or sacrifice does it ask of them? Starter answers: UNKNOWN for each until a buyer is asked. To choose the question to work on first, write beside each one what it would cost you to improve that answer, in money, counting your own hours at your hourly value from P13 (set it there first if it reads UNKNOWN). Work first on the question with the lowest cost. If costs tie, take them in this order: effort, then time until they see it, then how sure they are, then how big the result is. |
| Proof you can show today | Results, a before and after, a sample, or what a customer said, each with where it is kept. Starter: UNKNOWN. If you have none yet, write how you will get the first piece, such as one sample update made from your own notes. |
| Terms if it does not work | UNKNOWN until the owner writes them: the condition under which a buyer gets something back, written so the buyer and you can both check whether it happened, and what the buyer gets. Write only terms the business can keep. |
| Best customers | UNKNOWN until there are customers. Then list every customer with what they have paid you in total and whether they were easy to serve, by your own yes or no. Total paid counts both how long they stayed and how much they bought. Sort the list by total paid, go down it, skip anyone marked no, and stop when you have the number of best customers the owner chose: UNKNOWN until set. If totals tie at the cut-off, take the customer who started buying earlier. Write the traits those customers share. |
| What you will not sell | Anything that needs external sends or unsupported performance claims (from P01's constraints). |
| Safe default | The sheet describes the offer. It never promises a buyer a result the business cannot show evidence for. |

## Use now

3. Replace unsupported statements with `UNKNOWN`, then record the source or owner decision.
4. Run the row's completion or review check before depending on it.

## Adaptation notes

- Replace the neutral buyer and service only after the owner confirms them in P01.
- Take the buyer's words from a real conversation or message; keep the row `UNKNOWN` until you have one.
- Keep price `UNKNOWN` here until P10 shows what it earns.
- An offer change is a decision: record it in C07.
- Record how many best customers you chose to mark, and why, in C07.
